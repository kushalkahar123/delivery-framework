## "Why now" (02:30–14:00)

**Kushal:** So — why now? What changed?

**Priya:** Two things. One: the user base shifted under us. We launched as "Notion alternative for designers." That phrase was load-bearing. It made designers come. But over the last year — eighteen months really — the people who *stayed* aren't who I thought. They're not junior designers building portfolios. They're senior people. Mid-career. Design directors who need to pitch for a VP role. Product folks who need to make a board-level case for the work they led. Comms leads doing the same. The product accidentally became a *pitching* tool for people who already have a body of work.

**Kushal:** When did you notice?

**Priya:** Around October last year. I started doing manual welcome calls with new users — I do them for every paid signup. I kept hearing the same sentences. *"I'm putting myself up for a director role."* *"I'm leaving an agency and need to show what I led."* *"I have a board meeting where I need to present this work in narrative form, not slides."* None of those are "I'm a designer building a portfolio." Those are people who already have portfolios — they need to *narrate*.

**Kushal:** And the second thing?

**Priya:** The second thing is that I hired Marcus. In January. He's a brand consultant — he's helping me sharpen the positioning. He's been pushing me on this. He has a framework for it — he calls it the "narrative pillars" or something. I'll let him walk you through it. But what he's been hammering is: *every surface of your product is still talking like 2022 and your audience is somewhere in 2026 doing something you didn't build for*. He's right.

**Kushal:** Does Marcus's positioning framework exist as a deliverable? Like a deck or a doc?

**Priya:** Half. There's a Figma file with three "pillar" cards and a list of about twenty messages. I haven't shown it to anyone. He'll share it when we're ready.

*[Internal note: confirms the risk from the solo walk. Marcus has half a positioning artifact. P1 must integrate.]*

**Kushal:** Okay. Last question on intent, then we'll walk the product. What would *not* have made you write to us? What's not the problem?

**Priya:** *(pause)* The product itself. The editor. The reading experience. The mobile view. Those are good. I'm not asking for a re-architecture of the case-study editor.

**Kushal:** Good. Anything else "not the problem"?

**Priya:** The visual style. Marcus and I will eventually do a small brand refresh on the wordmark and palette, but that's a separate project. I want to keep that out of this.

*[Internal note: protected zones surfacing. Good. Capture in scope hypothesis.]*

**Kushal:** Let's walk it. Share your screen. Start where a new visitor would start.

**Priya:** *(shares loomwell.studio homepage)*

**Kushal:** And in the first ten seconds of a visitor looking at this, what's the message they should walk away with?

**Priya:** That this is the place to turn your work into a *pitch*. That Loomwell turns a body of work into a story that wins you the next role.

**Kushal:** Is that what this says?

**Priya:** No. It says we're a tool. It doesn't say what the tool does for you. It doesn't say who it's for. It doesn't say what the outcome looks like.

**Kushal:** Scroll. What's the next fold say?

**Priya:** *"Built for designers."* — yeah, that's wrong.

**Kushal:** Why?

**Priya:** Because half my users aren't designers anymore. And the half that are aren't junior. "Built for designers" reads like a portfolio tool. Loomwell isn't a portfolio tool.

**Kushal:** Scroll. Features section. What's the framing?

**Kushal:** What would the outcome list look like?

**Priya:** *(pauses, thinking out loud)* Something like — *"Pitch for a senior role with a story, not a screenshot."* *"Walk an investor through three years of work in one read."* *"Make your portfolio the thing that gets you in the room."* That.

## Synthesizing — structural vs surface vs brand-coherence (45:00–55:00)

**Kushal:** Let me read back what I think I'm hearing. Tell me where I'm wrong.

The product itself — editor, reading experience, mobile — you'd protect.

The visual design — the typography, the palette, the wordmark — you'd protect, with a small refresh coming separately.

What's broken is the **brand coherence**: voice, microcopy, positioning, audience framing. The product surfaces are still talking to a 2022 audience using 2022 language. *And* underneath that there's a **structural** problem too — there's no place on the marketing site where the positioning can live. No "who this is for" page. No way for the best case studies to be visible to a new visitor.

So this is mostly brand-coherence work — voice, tone, narrative across surfaces. With real structural work to make room for it. Surface work is light.

Is that right?

**Priya:** That's *exactly* right. And I want to flag — I've been calling this a "redesign" and that word might have been misleading. I don't think this is a visual redesign.

**Kushal:** Good — I think we agree. We use "Redesign" to mean *the live product needs to change*, regardless of whether the change is visual, structural, or narrative. Yours is mostly narrative with structural support.
